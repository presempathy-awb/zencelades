# Andrew's basket — occupied $3,000 DIY concept

Current decision: a person inside the sphere, a circular seat on a low
straight-tube triangle, and three webbing legs over the skin collecting at a
small top ring, swivel and halyard. Fabrication at denhac, materials paid and
labor in-kind. This is P036's chosen attachment concept, superseding the free
rope bridle in P033. P037–P040 specify **one common holder** with an aerial-rig
mode and a detachable three-leg base that looks like a landed rover or lunar
lander. **P041 removes the wooden platform.** Camera arms can attach to the triangle.
The actual sphere and overhead support are not selected.

## What the models show

`seed-zorb` shows the common ring/triangle holder on detachable lander legs. `basket-webbing`
shows the same basket with three broad lifting-webbing paths, collector,
swivel and halyard; its top marker means a host is required, not supplied.
`basket-tripod` adds an independent tripod envelope. `occupied-cradle` retains
the older tall rigid-frame alternative for comparison.

| Common-holder configuration | What changes beneath / above the triangle | Cost scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `basket-aerial-rig` | Four-legged aerial-rig envelope above, over-sphere webbing and upper hardware | Rig, rigging, stabilization and setup unpriced; no free loan assumed |
| `seed-zorb` | Detachable lower interfaces, splayed landing legs and broad footpads | Holder and leg materials share the ground allocation; actual quotes still needed |
| `basket-camera-arms` | One external camera-arm witness attached to a triangle corner | Illustrative placement; quantity, cameras and arms unpriced |

The lander treatment is a visual direction: compact triangular body, lower
attachment nodes, splayed struts and broad pads. It has no driving mechanism,
deployable suspension or self-leveling system. The model draws interfaces to
be designed, not proven quick-release joints. Legs would be removed for the
hanging configuration; the shared holder and its camera mounting locations stay.

All are layout studies with schematic member widths and hardware, not fabrication
drawings. The model test checks that the actual ring and triangle geometry is
identical across installation modes. A common geometric interface is not proof
that the joints safely transfer the different loads.

The camera study keeps equipment external and below the equator. A camera moving
with the holder may help keep its relative view stable, but shell distortion,
focus, glare and entry clearance need an optical trial. Camera mass and leverage
join the lifted payload. Secondary retention, cable strain relief and rotation
around the swivel need design; a cable service-loop sketch is not a slip ring
or permission for unlimited rotation. No camera quantity or live-feed software
has been selected by this attachment idea.

For aerial-rig fit, the [X-POLE height chart](https://xpole.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/A-Frame-Footfall-August-2024.pdf)
tops out at 3.480 m measured to the bar's top. That is already below this nominal
3 m sphere study's collector-plus-swivel top of 3.73 m, before a hoist or useful
clearance. The generic larger rig in the model is not an X-POLE product and is
not priced. A borrowed or rented rig needs actual usable height, leg clearance,
total payload and outdoor-use confirmation.

The nominal sphere is 3 m outside diameter; the inner 2 m envelope and seated
person are illustrative. The cutaway is a visual section, not an entry opening.
An actual entry, ventilation, seam map, skin/contact behavior and support during
deflation must come from the selected sphere and completed support design.

The intended load chain is sphere/occupant → distributed underside contact →
seating loop → triangle and its joints → webbing → collector → swivel →
halyard/hoisting system → approved host. This is not a calculation: load also
enters the skin wherever tensioned webbing bends around it. Three straps do not
guarantee one-third load each as the person moves or the shell deforms.

Keeping rigid basket members below the equator avoids tall metal uprights
across the projection surface. Webbing still masks light, casts shadows from
some angles, and can alter the surface. “No projection shadows” remains an
optical goal to test. “Nothing touches seams” remains a contact-routing
requirement; the model has no measured seam map. Loss of inflation could let
the sphere settle through the ring: a suitable broad underside support and
retrieval arrangement remain unresolved, not drawn as an imaginary working floor.

## Six-foot ring versus triangle

These are geometry checks on Andrew's nominal inputs, not structural sizing.
The “six-foot” donor label may describe overall diameter rather than the tube
centerline; the study uses a nominal centerline circle pending measurement.

| Corner radius from triangle center | Equilateral side | Inradius | Six-foot nominal circle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.50 m | 2.598 m | 0.750 m | Overhang at each side midpoint: 164.4 mm |
| 1.80 m | 3.118 m | 0.900 m | Overhang at each side midpoint: 14.4 mm |
| 1.8288 m | 3.168 m | 0.9144 m | Centerlines just enclose; no tube or joint clearance |

The side-length range in Andrew's prompt is consistent with an equilateral
triangle. Fully enclosing the ring is a different condition from seating it
across three supported interfaces. The current 1.80 m model deliberately shows
the slight overhang and schematic interfaces. Actual tube widths, interfaces
and deformation must decide the fit; enlarging the triangle is not automatically
the right repair.

The straps use tangent–arc–tangent paths over the nominal sphere, rather than
straight chords passing through it. A small display offset avoids overlapping
pixels; it does not represent a real air gap. Proposed free-bridle corner and
apex dimensions alone do not prove clearance: their height relative to the seat
and the actual sphere radius matter too.

## What to salvage

A six-foot trampoline ring is a **candidate curved-stock donor**, not a rated
lifting assembly. One manufacturer-authored [six-foot trampoline manual,
distributed by Home Depot](https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/4b/4bfde4ff-839e-40bb-b52d-e19f43f49c8e.pdf)
shows six frame tubes, separate leg supports, springs and assembly joints. That
particular product is for a child on a ground-supported domestic trampoline.
Its original support arrangement and rating do not validate an adult sphere
basket suspended from three corners. It does not identify Andrew's donor.

Compare a measured donor against known steel remnants and a purpose-rolled
ring. Record section, wall, holes, bends, corrosion, existing welds and every
sectional joint. Have the actual load path reviewed before choosing which
pieces carry the occupied assembly. Scrap of unknown condition can still be
useful for a layout mockup or non-load-bearing scenery without pretending its
capacity is known. No free donor or salvage credit has been booked.

[Altitude Steel's drop/remnant area](https://www.altitudesteel.com/carbon-supply)
sells by weight; no current price or inventory quote is published for this
basket. This is a concrete local material lead. Andrew's bender and denhac
access are user-confirmed resources; denhac also documents its
[TIG welder](https://denhac.org/wiki/primeweld-tig225x). Tool availability and
authorization are shop-specific, and no appointment or stock reservation has
been made. Odd Todd's participation remains to be agreed.

Labor is in-kind; tools are already available. Every paid amount is a ceiling
to test against a quote, not a claim of availability. In particular, no suitable
sphere at $1,200 delivered has been found. The review/coverage provision is not
an actual offer. Shared power and volunteer staffing remain to be arranged.

The suspended version still needs priced webbing, connectors, collector,
swivel, halyard/controlled lowering, a real support, stabilization, assembly
review and operating/retrieval arrangements. Their incremental cost is unknown,
not zero. Fitting that version under $3,000 would require actual savings,
documented donated equipment/services, or reallocating the existing total.
Possible fundraising and Andrew's contribution change who pays, not the cap.
The ground holder and its detachable legs must share the $350 materials
allocation; that is a ceiling to test, not a promise of sufficient stock or
hardware. Camera arms and the suspended setup need additional priced choices.
Reallocate within $3,000 after quotes, preserving the same holder in both modes.

No water, truck mount, internal cameras, occupied haze or purchased/rented
projection is in this allocation. A projector already committed as a loan can
be considered later; ownership was not confirmed. The hazer is owned, but the
current research only supports investigating an empty-shell optical trial.

# P036 — Three webbing legs over the sphere

Andrew's prompt:

> Lift over the top, not around. Three webbing straps from the triangle corners up and over the sphere to a small top ring, then swivel and halyard. Load path: loop, triangle, straps, top ring. Soft webbing on the skin is where restraint belongs, nothing rigid rises above the equator, no projection shadows. Rope bridles straight from the corners would press on the sphere unless the corners are 2.2 m out and the apex 2.6 m above centre, which eats rig height.
> - Triangle size: corners 1.5 to 1.8 m from centre (2.6 to 3.1 m sides) put the straps at a good angle.

Use this as the current attachment concept, superseding P033's separate rope
bridle and retaining straps. Model a seating loop, triangle, three broad webbing
paths contacting the sphere, a small top ring, swivel and halyard. No rigid
basket uprights rise above the sphere's equator. The external overhead support
remains a separately required and unpriced system.

Treat dimensions as proposed layout inputs, not engineering-approved sizes.
Verify circle/triangle fit and shell/path geometry. A six-foot circle has a
0.9144 m radius; an equilateral triangle's inradius is half its circumradius,
so 1.5–1.8 m corner radii do not fully enclose that circle without overhang or
another supporting arrangement. Webbing in contact under tension also loads
the skin: softness does not remove compression. Projection shadows and seam
clearance require an actual optical/contact test. No structural sizing, WLL,
hoist selection, attachment rating or occupied-operation approval is implied.

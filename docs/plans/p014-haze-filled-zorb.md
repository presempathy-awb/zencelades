## Findings and design judgment

Haze is worth comparing for a luminous cloud or icy atmosphere inside an empty
sphere. It is not yet supported as a replacement for the projection surface in
the concept renderings. Ordinary projected light scatters along its path through
a particle volume; I expect visible beams, overlapping imagery and lower
contrast as density increases. That is an optical inference, not a measured
result for this zorb. Published FogScreen work obtains recognizable images by
confining particles to a thin plane with surrounding stable airflow; a filled
sphere does not reproduce that geometry. [Mid-air display
experiments to create novel user interfaces](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11042-009-0280-1).

| Arrangement | Potential artistic use | What remains unresolved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Haze throughout an empty inner cavity | Glowing ocean, storm, plume or cloud effects | Image contrast, visible projector cones, density variation and clearing time |
| Haze confined between two transparent skins | A luminous shell around a clear inner space | Whether the actual sphere has an isolated air jacket; manufacturer approval; shell thickness, near/far-side image overlap, seams and cleaning access |
| Translucent projection skin with clean air | Recognizable moon surface, cracks, face or other mapped image | Material sample, gain, viewing angles and curved-surface optical test |

The second option is the most interesting interpretation of Andrew's proposal,
but its construction and performance are unconfirmed. No occupant is included
in the first prototype. Rosco's screen guidance describes the influence of
surface material and viewing geometry; a purpose-made diffusion sample is a
useful control in the comparison, not a guarantee that an arbitrary coating
will work. Do not paint or coat an existing inflatable without material
approval. [Rosco screen guidance](https://jp.rosco.com/ja/node/492).

## Haze added during normal inflation

Water-based theatrical fog can contain glycol or glycerin droplets; it is an
aerosol, not simply clean humid air. Use only the fluid specified for the
selected machine. [Rosco's explanation of fog generation](https://spectrum.rosco.com/how-a-fog-machine-works).

Andrew's normal blower handles inflation; the owned hazer adds the optical
effect partway through filling. Codex's earlier emphasis on using a hazer as
an inflator did not describe Andrew's intent. No connection method or approved
interface has been established. Compatibility questions concern the actual
machine/fluid, sphere materials, heat and the point of introduction.
Rosco's V-Hazer is a reference example, not the identified owned machine. Its manual
requires open output space, warns against enclosing the machine and against
continuous discharge onto nearby surfaces because fluid can re-condense. Its
compressed-air fitting is for cleaning, not evidence of an inflation function.
This manual does not approve a haze-filled inflatable. [V-Hazer manual,
printed pages 4–6 and 15](https://cn.rosco.com/sites/default/files/content/resource/2019-01/VHazer_FogMachine_UserGuide_v1_rev3.pdf).

Unknowns include TPU/PVC and seam compatibility, deposits on the inside skin,
cleanability of a sealed jacket, optical uniformity, leakage into an occupied
space, and actual fluid/power/maintenance costs. Hazer acquisition is $0 because
Andrew owns it; no new operating-cost estimate or equipment search is needed
for this clarification.

## Occupied use is a separate decision

Do not put a person in the haze test. In particular, the CPSC's warning concerns
airtight water-walking balls, where oxygen depletion, carbon-dioxide buildup
and lack of an internal exit can be fatal. It must not be generalized to every
double-wall zorb, but the depicted enclosed person requires confirming the
actual construction before claiming suitability. A hazer does not establish
breathing-air supply, ventilation or safe occupancy. [CPSC warning,
March 31, 2011](https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2011/Consumer-Alert-CPSC-Warns-of-Deadly-Danger-with-Water-Walking-Balls?language=en).

## Proposed next experiment, not executed

Codex recommends an unoccupied, small optical mock-up using the owned hazer
before committing the optical concept to the full-size installation.
First establish a manufacturer-supported arrangement for any haze
introduction; keep the machine outside and retain its required clearances and
ventilation. Compare clear enclosure, light haze and a translucent sample with
the same projector/content and ambient light. Photograph several viewing
angles; assess recognizable detail, cloud quality, near/far-side overlap,
clearing and deposits. A cloud-only result remains a viable alternative art
direction; it does not meet the original sharp 360-degree mapping goal by
itself. No pressure setting, exposure duration or improvised hose design is
specified by this research.

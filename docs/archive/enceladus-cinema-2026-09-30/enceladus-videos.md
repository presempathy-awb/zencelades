## Start here: above the ice

**Above the ice: Enceladus** is a 44.267-second silent film at 1920 × 1080 / 30 fps.
It combines NASA's south-pole overview and geyser fly-through, slows the icy
surface pass, adds a closer view inside the jets, then pulls away from the pole.
The rings and Saturn appear over the ice. Every segment is labeled as scientific
visualization with an imagined camera path and the full NASA laboratory credit.

The new film uses public source animation; no paid generation service was used.
It is an imagined surface experience, not footage from a camera standing on the
moon. Three-dimensional views here mean rendered spatial scenes or measured jet
models. The collection is flat video, not a stereoscopic headset or spherical
360-video package; “360” in E12 means the model rotates through a complete turn.

## Credits and reuse

The [source register](../../../assets/enceladus-videos/catalog.json) records the exact
credit line, download URL, classification, byte length, SHA-256, codec, resolution,
duration and immutable locator for every source and derived motion asset.

[NASA SVS's reuse guidance](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/help/) declares its content
public domain unless otherwise noted and separately flags licensed music.
The [JPL media policy](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/jpl-image-use-policy/) requires
credit and has third-party and endorsement exceptions. The
[Webb copyright page](https://webbtelescope.org/copyright) resolves to
[NASA media guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/).
These are source-specific reuse records, not a new blanket license for every
agency or outside contributor.

E01–E05 credit NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center Conceptual Image Lab. E06–E07
credit NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center and NASA/JPL/Space Science Institute.
Cassini image sequences retain the source's NASA/JPL/Space Science Institute
credit, including Caltech where supplied. E11 retains the full Leah Hustak
(STScI), NASA, ESA, CSA, Gerónimo Villanueva and Alyssa Pagan (STScI) credit.
E12 credits NASA/JPL-Caltech/Space Science Institute: its original NASA movie
link is missing from the migrated page, so the original NASA movie was retrieved
from [the American Astronomical Society's credited copy](https://aasnova.org/2015/10/02/geysers-from-the-tiger-stripes-of-enceladus/).

Cassini sequences are observations assembled into movies/time-lapses. Their
captions retain historical interpretation and viewing/exposure limitations.
The subsurface ocean, vents and close surface camera path in the NASA conceptual
animation are visualizations. The data-driven model includes uncertain vectors;
it does not make every geyser orientation an exact measurement.

## Recommended visual sequence

1. **Experience:** the strongest landed concept image. Caption it as generated
   concept art; it communicates the participant becoming a moon.
2. **Physical arrangement:** the actual 3D lander cutaway. Show the person,
   entrance, soft inner floor, padded loop, triangular holder and three heads.
3. **Scale and placement:** orthographic views with dimension lines. The entire
   assembly matters: the optical arms exceed 10 ft across in either sphere size.
4. **Assembly logic:** the frame-only view. Show what is shared and what detaches.
5. **Optional variant:** aerial suspension and size comparison, clearly labeled
   alternatives. Prefer a short coherent set to several competing main designs.

The technical images come from editable 3D geometry. The photorealistic concept
images are AI-generated. Neither is a photograph of a completed prototype.
The large cutaway is a drawing convention; the smaller round tunnel is the
proposed actual entrance. Its 680 mm diameter is a provisional model input,
not a selected product specification or validated access dimension.

## What successful-application guidance adds

[Burning Man's Honoraria FAQ](https://burningman.org/black-rock-city/bring-your-art/art-grants-programs/brc-honoraria/)
accepts sketches through 3D renderings to make a proposal understandable, asks
for disclosure of AI-generated imagery, and recommends additional visual methods
when AI images are used. This supports pairing the atmospheric concept image
with actual model views. These are Burning Man's rules, not Love Burn's upload limits.

[NYFA's work-sample guidance](https://www.nyfa.org/knowledge-base/nailing-the-application-start-strong-with-quality-work-samples/)
uses successful applications to show the value of a strong opening image,
cohesive sequencing, scale, context and process. Its Brigitta Varadi example
connects material/process images to the resulting installed work. For Zencelades,
that means experience, complete assembly, size, then a revealing detail.
This is a communication lesson, not evidence that any image sequence guarantees funding.

Prior funded-art and later-presentation examples remain in `assets/grant-sources.json`:
Shrumen Lumen, Firmament and The Space Whale have identified 2016 Honoraria award
sources. Museum of the Moon and Sonic Sphere are useful form/experience references;
this project has not established a Burning Man grant for either. Do not label
published promotional images as the exact attachments those artists submitted.

## A 75–90 second proposal-video outline

- **0–15 seconds:** Andrew introduces Zencelades and the idea of a human carrying
  an ocean within, becoming Enceladus through light.
- **15–35 seconds:** Show the landed concept and real 3D cutaway. Explain how one
  participant enters, sits, and shares the experience with people outside.
- **35–55 seconds:** Show the triangle, padded loop, detachable legs and projector
  arms. Describe the smaller-budget DIY approach, owned tools and planned in-kind labor.
- **55–75 seconds:** Explain the requested support and what still needs proving:
  exact sphere, occupied-use requirements, optical trial and final quotes. Present
  suspension and live portrait overlay as conditional features.
- **75–90 seconds:** Close on the community experience and the clearest view of
  the project. Keep the language personal and specific.

The existing cinematic may illustrate the idea, but the form specifically values
hearing from the artist. No video recording or public publication was performed.

## Placement and permit wording

The form asks about the whole art installation exceeding 10 ft in height or
diameter and separately about ballast/ground anchors. The 2.5 m sphere alone has
a 2.95 m top at the current centre height, but the arms and any aerial host make
the complete installation larger. Do not answer the size question from sphere
diameter alone or claim the smaller sphere avoids permit review. The form directs
uncertain projects to coordinate inclusion in the event permit; it tells artists
not to apply independently for a permit.

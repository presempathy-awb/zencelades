Love Burn 2027 application · prepared September 30, 2026

A moon you can
move with your body.

A 2.5 m walk-in zorb, lightly frosted, stands on lander legs or hangs at eye level from the artist’s own aerial rig. Two 3,600-lumen projectors paint it with fractured ice, the hidden ocean and its plumes. Climb in through the tunnel, sit on the soft floor, say “I am the man on the moon” (or woman, or whatever you are), and two cheap phone cameras inside put you on it: your live face, blown up to the size of the moon, fades in under the ice, the ice fractures around you, the hidden ocean floods through, plumes rise from where you sit, and the moon slowly takes itself back. Haze in the air outside makes the beams visible. One person inside at a time, a crew member at the tunnel, a switch at the door and a stop switch at the seat.

These schematics are the Tier 1 build for the application: one person inside at a time, dry land, projectors on stands, no engineering beyond a fabricated steel triangle and an aerial rig used within its manual. The concept art shows the one-unit upgrade with projector heads on arms from the triangle; the over-water version remains a future ambition with its own approvals.

Hanging it: the sphere side and the support side

The moon is the zorb. A TPU zorb weighs tens of kilograms (a 3 m one 80 to 100 kg) and has no rated top point, so nothing hangs from its skin: it seats in a padded steel loop at the centre of a fabricated triangle, lifts on a three-line bridle through a rated swivel, and a hand winch with a brake on the rig leg does the raising and lowering. Its skin has to diffuse light, frosted or white or with a diffusing liner; the October bench test proves that before anything else is bought.

Cheap static metal, done right, is a lighting goalpost. Two 14 ft ProX crank stands (220 lb each, TUV certified, about $316 to $400 each on sale) and a 10 ft truss span make a rated, resellable goalpost that fits in the truck and cranks the moon down in wind in a minute: $1,010 to $1,824 with contingency, artist-funded, used only if the owned rig fails the fit check. A custom weld costs about the same, is rated by nobody and cannot be lowered.

Plates 2 and 3 · A tree, or the ground.

If something is not allowed.

No aerial rig or truss
The moon lands. Three pinned legs on the steel triangle stand it like a landed probe (NASA’s Enceladus concept is an Orbilander) with its centre at the same 1.7 m and its top just under 10 ft: same projectors, same cameras, same show, nothing overhead, nothing to climb, sandbags on the foot pads. The landing can also be the nightly show beat.
The truck
Transport only: it unloads at the placement and parks at Miami Marine Stadium like any car; the kit is hand-carried in one trip with three people. Parking it at the camp as scenery is a later option, not part of the 2027 ask. What Love Burn allows.
No event power
An inverter generator under 5,000 W at the artist’s cost, or a battery station for intermittent nights.
No haze
The piece runs without it; the beams are a bonus.
No tree, no shoreline
Never assumed; rig or lander legs on dry land.
Wind
Lowered and strapped in a minute; above the second limit, deflated and covered for the night.

Ledger and the ask.

What a Love Burn grant usually is

From Love Burn’s own finance disclosures: LB2024 paid $440,092 in cash art grants across 210 projects, about $2,096 per project; LB2023 paid $387,071 across 149, about $2,598. (The 2026 and 2025 links on that page currently open the 2024 and 2023 files.)

The form sorts requests into tiers: Spark to $599, Seed $600 to $3,000 for “simple to medium builds, portable art, or small interactive pieces”, Feature $3,000 to $15,000, Anchor above. Ask: $3,000 cash as a Seed Grant, plus 3 crew tickets and one power circuit, itemised against the Tier 1 list. It covers the low build in full; the artist covers the rest and every in-kind line.

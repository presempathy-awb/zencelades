A low cradle or hemisphere. A smaller moon keeps the experience visible while reducing support height, wind area and the surface to illuminate.

Receiver crane

Aerial rigs: check the space
inside the legs.

Illustrative height budget: 3.4 m globe + 0.3 m ground gap + 0.6 m bridle + 0.2 m hardware + 0.3 m movement allowance = 4.8 m. These are comparison assumptions, not safe-clearance specifications.

A 12-foot rig is too short for that example. A nominal 16-foot rig has almost no spare height. A 20-foot rig deserves a proper fit study, including its actual attachment point, sloping legs, suspension assembly and audience footprint.

The X-POLE documentation rules it out for the original full-size concept on geometry and intended-use grounds. A headline weight rating cannot resolve either.

The suspension chain

Assessed support → compatible suspension hardware → a sphere-specific spreader or cradle → the unoccupied scenic object. Handles and inflation fittings are not established lifting points. A rigger specifies the raising/lowering method, retention, inspections and recovery.

Keep projector supports separate where practical. Sphere motion otherwise changes both loading and image registration. No internal cameras are needed for a prerecorded moon/ocean loop or an external visitor control.

A tree is a living structure.
It needs its own assessment.

Uplift’s tree guidance calls for an arborist and an experienced rigger. Hidden damage, the particular limb or union, roots, load direction and changing condition matter. Two trees and a nearly horizontal line can increase anchor forces dramatically.

Love Burn addresses damage to flora and park infrastructure. That is not permission to use a particular tree. Obtain a written site decision; do not assume straps, stakes, drilling or pruning are allowed.

The tree option may save frame material and still cost more after assessment, rigging, protection and inspection. Keep a complete ground-supported fallback in the proposal.

A smaller moon changes
more than its price.

A 2 m sphere has about 35% of the frontal area of the 3.4 m concept. That reduces the area exposed to wind and the surface to illuminate. Removing water, motion and internal capture further reduces separate engineering problems.

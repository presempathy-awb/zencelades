An ocean within a moon. A person within the light.

Step inside another world.

Zencelades invites a person to become a moon. A seated participant inside a soft sphere becomes a living presence among ice, ocean and light.

Inspired by Enceladus, Saturn’s ocean moon, the sculpture brings an intimate human encounter to Love Burn’s SHOWTIME! theme. Friends gather outside; someone inside becomes part of the show. Both viewpoints matter.

Proposal submitted, as reported by Andrew. The submission acknowledgment has been received. A grant award, placement and permission to operate still require an official Art Committee Offer Notice. The work is in design development.

The audience makes the show.

The intended experience is a supervised turn inside a softly padded sphere. Projected imagery moves across its skin while people gather around it. Watching from outside is a complete way to participate.

Two inexpensive or existing phones are budgeted to blend a consenting participant’s live image with the moon. Capture, mapping, participant controls and the final optical setup still need to be demonstrated.

Made for a shared moment

The physical idea is deliberately small and handmade: a moon that looks as though it has landed, with its entrance facing the gap between two legs. The third leg sits behind the sphere. Soft contact surfaces and an open approach are central to the design.

The main proposal uses two purchased projectors on independent stands. Arms attached to the triangle, short-throw heads and a third projector are alternate designs. Full-sphere coverage has not been demonstrated.

Explore the configurations

A common frame, a clear entrance.

Love Burn sphere
2.5 m nominal outside diameter in the proposal; 3.0 m remains an alternate study. The actual entry tunnel, usable interior and manufacturer’s conditions need checking against the model.
Common holder
A low metal triangle around a padded circular support ring, with a soft inner floor and a seated participant. Model cutaways reveal the interior; the cutaway is not the actual entrance.
Lander
Detachable legs: two beside the entrance, one at the rear. Foot pads, ballast, joints and ground conditions require design and physical validation.
Main aerial design
Over-sphere webbing rises to a collector ring and swivel beneath the existing aerial rig assumed by the proposal. Occupied suspension depends on product compatibility, engineered load paths, suitable rigging, host capacity, access and rescue provisions.
Light and atmosphere
Two purchased HD146X projectors with stands and covers, plus two phone feeds to the existing laptop. Andrew’s owned hazer is an external effect behind/below the sphere; its placement and operating clearances still need checking.

These are design studies. A drawing, donor ring or nominal hardware size does not establish a safe working load. Fabrication specifications and occupied operation need qualified review and physical verification.

Build toward $3,000.

$3,000 is the total cash ceiling. Grant support, Andrew’s contribution and any fundraiser all contribute to that same total.

The main estimate now follows Claude’s application ledger: two purchased projectors, phone mounts and controls, stand covers, rigging, the fabricated holder and lander legs, with 20% contingency. Its lower estimate fits the target; the higher estimate needs reductions before committing to the build.

Open the budget and alternatives

Build around what is real

Andrew owns the hazer and a pipe bender and has access to Denhac. The application assumes the existing sphere, aerial rig, laptop and network, with fabrication labor in-kind. Replacement purchases and any unconfirmed commitments need explicit prices.

The studio starts with this Love Burn design and its itemized purchase budget. Other supports, LED-only allocations, projector arms and rental packages are grouped as Alternate designs.

From proposal to a real encounter.

The next work is to select the actual sphere, test its contact with the holder, price the chosen light setup, and resolve the occupied installation with the appropriate specialists and event team. The submission acknowledgment also asks the build team to complete a separate theme-camp application and watch for Ops coordination.

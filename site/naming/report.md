## Directions worth saying aloud

### fathomoon
fathom + moon; FATH-uh-moon

Fathom means both a measure of depth and an act of understanding. Blending its final m with moon joins the ocean within the moon to the audience’s act of looking into it.

This is the strongest poetic direction in this batch. It leaves room for body, water and cosmic scale without making the zorb apparatus the whole identity; the extra syllable is the price of that richness.

### selenerime
selene + rime; seh-LEE-neh-rime

Selene brings the moon’s mythic identity and rime brings a surface of frozen water. Both roots stay audible, giving the installation a quiet ice-and-moon character.

A poetic direction for Enceladus’s bright shell, with room for the hidden ocean story underneath. Selene’s pronunciation may need introducing, and the fun and human movement would come through the artwork rather than the name alone.

### causticuddle
caustic + cuddle; KAW-stih-KUD-ul

In optics, a caustic is the bright pattern formed when light is concentrated by a curved surface; cuddle supplies warmth and bodily closeness. Sharing c gives the pair a playful seam.

This is a deliberately odd lensing-and-affection direction. The optical meaning is highly relevant, but the everyday harsh or chemical meaning of caustic is a real naming drawback.

### apsisoma
apsis + soma; ap-sih-SOH-ma

An apsis is a near or far turning point along an orbit; soma means body. Sharing s gives the name a connection between celestial motion and the performer’s presence.

This is an expressive body-and-orbit direction with a smooth spoken rhythm. Both roots are less familiar than moon or bubble, so its meaning would benefit from a short accompanying description.
